# India Dairy Regulatory Research Register

**Status:** Research register — not a legal opinion  
**Last reviewed:** 2026-11-19  
**Purpose:** Identify regulatory topics that may affect product requirements and preserve source evidence. Applicability must be determined for each tenant's actual activities, size, location, and legal profile.

## 1. Critical product-design principle

Do not assume that GST registration is universally required for every small dairy business, and do not assume that absence of GST registration removes all food-business obligations. Tax registration, food-business registration/licensing, local permissions, and other obligations are separate questions.

The system must record applicability and evidence separately rather than infer one registration from another. It must not block branch creation merely because a tenant has no GST registration.

## 2. FSSAI — milk producers and milk vendors advisory (11 March 2026)

An FSSAI advisory dated 11 March 2026 states that milk producers other than members of dairy cooperative societies who supply/sell their entire milk to the society, and milk vendors, are advised to mandatorily register with FSSAI before commencing or continuing operations. It also refers to periodic inspection of milk chillers and enforcement action for non-compliance.

**Product implications for investigation:**
- Business profile should support FSSAI registration/license details, validity, document evidence, and applicability status.
- The system should not automatically infer that a user is exempt merely because the business is small or not GST-registered.
- Where relevant, support chiller/equipment records and temperature/inspection logs.
- Registration status should support unknown, applicable/pending, valid, expired, and not-applicable states with recorded reason/evidence; final state taxonomy requires compliance review.
- The system should distinguish a reminder/control from a legal determination. It must not claim to provide a legal eligibility decision.

**Important caveat:** The advisory wording and applicability must be checked against the current official instrument, subsequent amendments/directions, and the tenant's facts before turning this into a hard validation or onboarding block.

Official source:
https://fssai.gov.in/upload/uploadfiles/files/Advisory%20dated%2011_03_2026%20related%20to%20Milk%20Producers%20and%20Milk%20vendors.pdf

## 3. FSSAI hygiene and dairy handling

FSSAI's hygiene materials describe food-safety management and hygienic practices across food-chain activities. Its hygiene requirements distinguish petty food operators from manufacturing/processing/packaging/storage/distribution and identify specific requirements for milk and milk products.

Potential product capabilities to evaluate:
- Food safety and hygiene checklist templates.
- Cleaning and sanitation schedules/logs.
- Chiller/storage temperature logs and exception alerts.
- Equipment and container cleaning records.
- Raw material receiving and acceptance checks.
- Batch/lot and product traceability where applicable.
- Corrective action, rejection, hold, and disposal records.
- Training and personnel competency records where applicable.

Do not impose manufacturing-level workflows on a tenant that only performs a different activity. The applicable requirements must be mapped by activity and legal classification.

Official sources:
- https://fssai.gov.in/inspection/hygiene-requirements
- https://www.fssai.gov.in/business/guidance
- https://fssai.gov.in/food-law/regulations

### 3.1 FSSAI — advisory on controlled-temperature transport/storage of perishable food commodities (15 October 2026)

FSSAI issued an advisory dated 15 October 2026 concerning handling, storage and transportation of perishable food commodities under controlled-temperature conditions. The advisory refers to commodities such as khoa, paneer, meat and fish and directs Food Business Operators handling the commodities within its stated scope to:
- Transport them only in clean, covered and hygienic vehicles/conveyances suitable for food.
- Maintain the prescribed temperature conditions throughout storage and transportation.
- Use food-grade packaging and suitable secondary packaging.
- Protect products from dust, moisture, pests, physical damage and cross-contamination during storage, loading, unloading and transportation.
- Maintain appropriate consignment records and traceability and produce invoices or relevant documents when required by the competent authority.

**Product implications for investigation:**
- Delivery/transport profiles must support product- and activity-specific vehicle suitability, hygiene/cleanliness checks, temperature evidence, food-grade packaging, loading/unloading condition checks, traceable consignment references and exception/hold records.
- These controls must be scoped by actual product, business activity and applicability. The advisory must not be translated into one universal temperature or checklist for all dairy SKUs, retail-only activities, raw milk and processed dairy products.
- Where a relevant control is legally mandatory, a Tenant cannot disable it through a lower-level preference. The application must retain source, effective date, jurisdiction, applicability and the verified rule/profile version.
- The advisory does not itself provide one numeric temperature for all commodities; do not hardcode one value based on this advisory.
- Legal applicability, amendments, other product-specific rules and any vehicle/transport documentation requirements must be reviewed against the current official source before compliance enforcement is implemented.

**Official primary source:** FSSAI, Advisory dated 15 October 2026, F. No. RCD-15001/11/2021-Regulatory-FSSAI:
https://www.fssai.gov.in/docs/food-law/advisory/Advisory%20dated%2015.10.2026_Transportation%20and%20storage%20of%20perishabe%20commodities.pdf

### 3.2 FSSAI Milk and Milk Products FSMS guidance — raw-milk transport and chilling examples (14 March 2019)

The FSSAI Milk and Milk Products FSMS Guidance Document describes raw-milk handling from village collection through MCC/BMC and transport to a processing unit. Its guidance states that raw milk should be transported from the village-level collection point to MCC/BMC/processing, as applicable, within four hours of milking and cooled as soon as practicable to 5°C or below. It also discusses chilling at collection/chilling centres, clean milk cans/containers and equipment, insulated containers/tankers and preservation of milk integrity during transport.

**Product implications for investigation:**
- A raw-milk workflow may need effective-dated collection time, milking time where known, chilling time, temperature readings, source device/manual method, container/tanker, dispatch/arrival and receiving test evidence.
- The values above are documented in an FSSAI sector FSMS guidance document. Do not present them as a universal statutory rule for every milk product, every process stage or every operating model. Confirm current Schedule 4/regulatory text, activity applicability and any later official amendments/clarifications before enforcing a hard dispatch block.
- Processed/perishable products such as paneer, khoa and packaged milk require their own applicable product/transport profiles; raw-milk guidance is not substituted for those rules.
- Preserve the exact guidance source/version and operational profile used in a decision.

**Official source:** FSSAI, Food Safety Management System Guidance Document for Milk and Milk Products, uploaded 14 March 2019:
https://mail.fssai.gov.in/upload/uploadfiles/files/Guidance_Document_Milk_14_03_2019.pdf

## 4. Milk procurement and quality operations

NDDB sources describe milk collection and procurement practices including producer identification, quantity measurement, sampling, fat/SNF testing, rate calculation, transaction receipts, chilling/storage, transportation, receiving checks, and processing.

Potential product capabilities:
- Supplier/farmer identity and membership/relationship type.
- Collection sessions and shifts.
- Quantity and measurement equipment details.
- Sample identification and test results.
- Rate charts and effective dates.
- Acceptance, rejection, and dispute/retest records.
- Supplier transaction receipt and settlement ledger.
- Chilling/storage and transport records where used.

These operational practices are domain evidence, not a statement that every small vendor must implement every step.

Sources:
- https://www.nddb.coop/ccnddb/milkipedia
- https://beta.nddb.coop/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Manual_Component_A_Strengthening_Milk_Procurement_Infrastructure.pdf

## 5. GST and tax topics — research required

No GST applicability conclusion is made in this register. Future research must separately establish:
- Registration threshold and compulsory-registration situations as applicable to the business's facts.
- Product classification and applicable tax treatment for relevant milk and dairy products.
- Exempt/taxable supply distinctions and mixed baskets.
- Tax invoice, bill of supply, receipt voucher, credit note, and debit note requirements where relevant.
- Place/time/value of supply and branch/legal-entity implications.
- E-invoicing and e-way bill applicability where relevant.
- Record retention and return/reporting needs.
- Whether a tenant's operating profile creates obligations independent of GST registration.

Use current CBIC/GST Council notifications, Acts, Rules, official FAQs/circulars, and Gazette publications. Record the date and exact provision. Do not derive legal rules from third-party summaries alone.

## 6. Other legal/regulatory research tracks

The following require separate evidence-based research before requirements are finalized:
- Legal Metrology: weighing instruments, packaged commodities, declarations, and measurement controls where applicable.
- Consumer protection and packaged-product information.
- State/local permissions and municipal requirements.
- Shops and Establishments and applicable employment/labour requirements.
- Payroll-related statutory deductions and benefits where applicable.
- Data protection, privacy notices, consent, access, retention, and breach handling.
- Payment-provider and banking terms for integrations and reconciliation.
- Transport, cold-chain, and vehicle-related obligations where relevant.
- Wastewater, environmental, and local pollution-control requirements for processing/manufacturing operations.

## 7. Compliance data model requirements (provisional)

The product should evaluate a generic compliance-record pattern rather than hard-code one registration type into tenant identity:
- Compliance type and issuing authority.
- Applicability status and reason.
- Registration/license/reference number (optional according to type).
- Legal entity/business/branch/location scope.
- Effective-from, expiry, renewal, and status dates.
- Supporting document and verification evidence.
- Verified by, verification date, and source.
- Reminder and review history.
- Change/audit history.

This is a candidate information model, not an approved schema. Sensitive identifiers must be protected by least-privilege access and appropriate retention controls.
